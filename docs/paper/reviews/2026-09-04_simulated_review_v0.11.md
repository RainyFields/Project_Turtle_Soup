# Simulated pre-submission peer review — iab2026 draft v0.11

**Reviewed artifact:** `docs/paper/iab2026.tex` (v0.11, commit 7b06d5d) + `figures/fig1_flatness.png`, `figures/fig2_geometry.png`.
**Process:** three mutually blind reviewers, each run in an isolated subagent context with the identical manuscript packet (the three files above, nothing else from the repository) and a preassigned emphasis brief (R1 quantitative soundness; R2 originality and claim–evidence alignment; R3 methodology, reproducibility, accessibility). Reports were frozen verbatim before comparison; the synthesis was written afterwards and was not shown to any reviewer. Generated with the nature-reviewer skill; this is a simulation for the authors, not an editorial decision.

## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript source and both main figures; appendices included.
- **Assessment boundary** Reviewers could not see the repository's data, scripts, plan, or logs; claims about what data exist were judged from the manuscript alone.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** Task failure in a 22-puzzle Turtle Soup grid is driven by ineffective exploration rather than budget or capability alone; two behavioral patterns (circling, abandoning) are identified; trajectory geometry is interpretive, not predictive; environments need an information audit.
- **Visible evidence base** E1 (198 games, per-round checkpoints), E2 (1,188 games), sustained-peak retention statistics, mixed-effects LRTs, dual-anchor drift analysis, one worked game.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Audit protocol data, pipeline specifications, token budgets, per-game pattern classification.

## Reviewer 1

- **Overall assessment**
This paper instruments a lateral-thinking game (Turtle Soup) with per-round forced-answer checkpoints and an embedding-space trajectory, and argues that the flat accuracy curve reflects an equilibrium of finding and losing rather than a budget or capability limit. The framing is unusually honest about its own null results, the worked game in Appendix B is genuinely illuminating, and the environment-audit point is well taken. However, the paper's inferential posture is inverted; the only formal statistics reported are for the null claims (LRTs, within-model correlations), while every positive headline claim in the abstract (retention 0.69 to 0.76 and its scale invariance, the 3.5-fold peak increase, the monotonic harm to 4B, the subscore dissociation) is a point estimate with no uncertainty, no test, and in several cases no stated estimator. The retention analysis in particular is exposed to scoring jitter that the paper itself documents (a rewording swinging the clue half from 70 to 0), and the two-round sustain guard does not remove the upward bias of a max statistic over 29 windows. The Oracle audit, on which the validity of every downstream number rests, is reported in six words. The core idea deserves publication, but the quantitative case is not yet established at the standard the paper's own carefulness elsewhere leads a reader to expect.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why**
Researchers in agent evaluation and interpretability who suspect endpoint scores hide process-level failure modes; the circling versus abandoning distinction is directly actionable for intervention design. Benchmark builders will also care about the provenance-enforced puzzle set, the under-determination covariate, and the Oracle-audit argument, which generalizes to any environment where a judge or oracle is the sole information channel.

- **Major strengths**
  - The checkpoint mechanism (forcing and scoring a complete story every round) is a genuinely informative instrument; the round-10 committed story in Table 1 being the exact solution, later abandoned, is a compelling existence proof that endpoint scores discard the decisive information.
  - The paper reports its own null results (H2 rejected, H3 unsupported) plainly and repositions the geometry as interpretive rather than predictive, rather than salvaging significance.
  - The dual-anchor robustness check (solution-aware versus surface-only, with the sign-flip count of 38 of 198 disclosed) is exactly the kind of self-audit most trajectory papers omit.
  - The provenance restriction to human-solved puzzles, with the under-determination measure deliberately decoupled from rounds used, avoids an obvious circularity in the accuracy-versus-rounds analysis.
  - Cross-family Oracle requirement and the harness-trap disclosure (truncated CoT read as questions) show real methodological maturity.

- **Major Concerns**

  - **R1-M1.** Severity Major. Blocking Yes. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract claim (1) and Section 5.1 ("models end with only 0.69--0.76 of what they had demonstrably reached... leaves retention unchanged"). Evidence pointer, Section 5.1 and Figure 1b; Appendix B ("the curve's round-to-round jitter is a scoring fact"). The retention statistic is never defined as an estimator. Is 0.69 to 0.76 a per-model ratio of mean final score to mean sustained peak, or a mean of per-game ratios? Per-game ratios are unstable when the denominator is near zero, which is the typical 4B case (sustained peak mean 0.084). No confidence interval accompanies any of the three per-model values, and "scale leaves retention unchanged" is asserted from three point estimates spanning 0.07 with no test of equality. Most importantly, the sustained peak is still a maximum over roughly 29 two-round windows of a noisy series; the paper itself documents that rewording alone swings the clue half from 70 to 0, so the max is biased upward and the final round is a single noisy draw, which mechanically produces retention below 1 even for an agent whose knowledge never changes. Why it matters, this is the paper's central positive finding; without a jitter-only null model, "finding and losing" cannot be distinguished from "measurement noise around a stable plateau." Resolution test, define the estimator; bootstrap CIs per model; and simulate a null in which per-round knowledge is constant and only the documented scoring jitter operates (resampling observed checkpoint sequences, or permuting within-game scores), then show observed retention falls outside that null at each scale, and test retention equality across scales directly.

  - **R1-M2.** Severity Major. Blocking Yes. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract claim (4) ("an unaudited Oracle collapses questions to 'irrelevant' and makes every game unwinnable") and the Results preamble. Evidence pointer, Section 4 "Prerequisite: the Oracle audit" and Section 5 ("audited at 95%; two logic samples per rating"); no supporting data appear anywhere in the body or appendices. Two problems. First, abstract claim (4) is presented as a finding, but the body contains no experiment, no numbers, and no example from the failed Oracle candidate; as written it is an anecdote promoted to a contribution. Second, the audit of the accepted Oracle is reported as "95%" with no denominator, no description of the held-out pairs, no reference standard, and no per-answer-class breakdown (accuracy on 是 versus 与此无关 matters enormously here). Why it matters, the Oracle is the sole information channel and the logic judge supplies 30 of 100 points; every downstream number inherits their validity, and at present that validity is not assessable. Resolution test, report the audit protocol (pair count, construction, reference labels, per-class accuracy, and the same numbers for at least one rejected candidate), and either supply the data behind the unwinnability claim or demote it from the abstract to a motivating observation.

  - **R1-M3.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract claim (3) ("mixed-effects models find no predictive power for stride or drift") and Section 5.3. Evidence pointer, Section 5.3, paragraph beginning "The mixed-effects test makes that precise." The model is underspecified and the null is overstated. The response variable is never named (final score or best checkpoint accuracy). The random-effects structure is puzzle intercept only, yet the 198 games are 22 puzzles x 3 models x 3 seeds, so puzzle-by-model clustering and seed structure are unmodeled, which can distort LRT p-values. ML versus REML for the LRTs is unstated. The response is bounded, visibly quantized (Figure 2 shows scores concentrated at a handful of values such as 0.03, 0.12, 0.30), and zero-inflated, and no diagnostics are offered for a linear mixed model on such data. Finally, the abstract says "no predictive power for stride or drift, p >= 0.20," but the body reports only the drift main effect (p = 0.39) and a stride x tier interaction (p = 0.20); a stride main-effect LRT appears nowhere, and no effect-size CIs or power considerations accompany a null claimed from n = 198 games on a coarse outcome. Why it matters, "no predictive power" is a strong universal negative resting on two tests of an underdescribed model. Resolution test, fully specify the model (response, estimation method, random-effects structure including puzzle x model), report the stride main-effect test, give coefficient CIs so the null is bounded rather than asserted, and either verify robustness with an ordinal or beta-family model suited to the quantized bounded response or justify the Gaussian LMM.

  - **R1-M4.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract claim (1) ("longer budgets monotonically hurt the smallest model, 0.050 to 0.004") and Section 5.2. Evidence pointer, Section 5.2 (175 of 396 4B games ended by token-budget exhaustion) and Section 6 ("A harness trap worth naming"). The paper's own limitation section states that under a too-small token budget a model's logged output is indistinguishable from an agent that cannot form a question, and that "decoding configuration is part of the environment." Yet 44% of the 4B games end by token exhaustion, and the abstract presents the 4B decline as a behavioral finding about the model. The per-round forced checkpoint (a complete story every round) plausibly consumes the budget faster at longer caps, which would make the monotone decline a harness artifact of exactly the kind the paper warns against. No test of the monotone trend is reported either. Why it matters, one of the abstract's headline numbers may measure the environment, by the paper's own audit principle. Resolution test, report 4B end accuracy conditioned on non-exhausted games, report the exhaustion rate by cap, state the token budgets, and show the decline survives (or reattribute it explicitly to the harness); add a trend test.

  - **R1-M5.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract claim (2) and Section 5.3 (circling versus abandoning as model-separating patterns). Evidence pointer, Section 5.3; Figure 1b caption ("18--20 of 66 per model" ended below half their peak). The two patterns are established by aggregate stride means, small counts (sustained peak >= 0.5 in 4 to 6 of 66 games per larger model, so under 10% of games ground "larger models abandon... reaching the solution mid-game"), and one worked example; there is no operational per-game classification, so no count of circling versus abandoning games exists. Moreover Figure 1b's own caption says 18 to 20 of 66 games per model, 4B included, ended below half their peak, so below-half-peak endings are equally common at 4B; the dichotomy as stated ("4B never finds a solution to lose") is then a magnitude difference dressed as a categorical behavioral difference. Why it matters, the two-pattern taxonomy is contribution (2) and drives the concluding claim that the two failure modes need opposite interventions. Resolution test, define per-game criteria for circling and abandoning (thresholds on late-game stride, sustained peak, and peak-to-end drop), report the resulting confusion table by model, and reconcile the 4B below-half-peak count with the "never finds" narrative.

  - **R1-M6.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract ("a two-part score whose clue-recall and causal-logic halves demonstrably measure different competences") and Section 5.2 "The two score halves dissociate." Evidence pointer, Section 5.2 and Appendix B ("If the two rankings had agreed... they do not"). The evidence offered is that each model earns a larger proportional share of the logic half than the clue half (e.g., 12.6/30 versus 7.2/70). Different marginal shares of two subscales with different ceilings, difficulties, and graders do not demonstrate different competences; two halves can differ in marginal difficulty while ranking games or models identically. The appendix asserts the rankings disagree but reports no correlation, per-game or per-model, between the halves. Judge reliability for the logic half is also unreported (two samples per rating are averaged, with no agreement statistic). Why it matters, "demonstrably measure different competences" is an abstract-level validity claim about the paper's released scoring instrument. Resolution test, report the per-game correlation (and rank correlation) between the two halves, within model, plus inter-sample agreement of the logic judge; revise the claim to what those numbers support.

- **Minor Comments**

  - **R1-m1.** Severity Minor. Axis technical soundness. Affected element, Section 5.3 pooled and within-model Spearman correlations. Evidence pointer, "within each model the correlation vanishes (|rho| <= 0.16)." With n = 66 per model, |rho| = 0.16 is compatible with modest true effects; "vanishes" overstates a nonsignificant estimate. Required correction, report CIs for the per-model correlations and rephrase as "not detected at this sample size."
  - **R1-m2.** Severity Minor. Axis technical soundness. Affected element, dual-anchor robustness paragraph, Section 5.3. Evidence pointer, "the mixed-effects conclusion holds under both (surface-only LRT p = 0.10)." A p of 0.10 with sign flips on 38 of 198 traces is weaker support than "the anchor changes traces, not the finding" implies; also only the drift test is reported under the surface anchor. Required correction, soften the wording and report the stride test under the surface-only anchor as well.
  - **R1-m3.** Severity Minor. Axis technical soundness. Affected element, under-determination measure, Section 3.2 and Appendix A. Evidence pointer, "sampling twelve cold guesses... taking the closest"; "one minus that best guess." The generating model for the twelve guesses and the scoring of "closest" are unspecified; if either uses the evaluated model family or the composite score, the covariate is coupled to the outcome apparatus. Also, r = -0.22 "n.s." with n = 22 puzzles cannot establish independence from surface length.   - **R1-m4.** Severity Minor. Axis technical soundness. Affected element, score granularity. Evidence pointer, Figure 2 (scores concentrated on a few values) and Table 1 (0.12, 0.91, 1.00). The composite appears coarsely quantized, presumably because clue recall on few-clue puzzles is near-binary; this bears on every continuous analysis. Required correction, report the attainable score support (clue counts per puzzle, score histogram) so readers can judge the granularity.
  - **R1-m5.** Severity Minor. Axis technical soundness. Affected element, the "scale" framing throughout. Evidence pointer, Section 5 preamble (Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.6-27B, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B). The three models differ in version (3.5 versus 3.6) and architecture (the 397B is MoE with 17B active parameters), and 27B is non-monotonic on stride (0.114 versus 397B's 0.088); three confounded points cannot support scaling language such as "scale raises the peaks 3.5-fold." Required correction, replace "scale" with "model tier" or acknowledge the version and architecture confounds explicitly.
  - **R1-m6.** Severity Minor. Axis readability. Affected element, Section 5 preamble versus Section 5.2. Evidence pointer, "zero failed games" alongside "175 of its 396 games ending by token-budget exhaustion." The terminology invites contradiction; define "failed game" (harness failure) versus exhaustion as a game outcome. Required correction, one clarifying clause.
  - **R1-m7.** Severity Minor. Axis technical soundness. Affected element, retention computation for early-committing games. Evidence pointer, Section 5.1 (11 of 66 games committed by 27B, mean round 11.1). Whether the "final" score for a game that commits at round 11 is the commit-round score or a round-30 forced checkpoint is unstated, and the choice changes the retention statistic. Required correction, state the rule and report retention with and without early-committing games.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
R1-M1 (retention estimator, uncertainty, and jitter null) and R1-M2 (Oracle and judge audit reporting, and the unsupported abstract claim 4) are blocking. R1-M3 through R1-M6 must also be resolved for the abstract to be a fair summary of what the body establishes; at present every tested claim is a null and every positive claim is untested.

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
Originality is solid; per-round forced-answer checkpoints plus embedding-trajectory readouts on a provenance-controlled lateral-thinking set is a fresh combination, and the finding-versus-keeping decomposition is a genuinely new lens on plateaued curves. Scientific importance is real but currently contingent, since the actionable conclusion (opposite interventions for circling and abandoning) rests on a taxonomy that is not yet operationalized per game. Technical soundness is the weak axis, as detailed above; the paper tests its nulls rigorously and its positives not at all, and the measurement chain (Oracle, judge, quantized composite) is insufficiently audited in the text. Readability is high for an interdisciplinary audience; the worked game and the plain-language treatment of the anchor caveat are model examples, though the abstract packs more numeric claims than the body can currently carry.

- **Recommendation posture**
Major revision needed. The instrument and the framing are worth publishing at this workshop, but the headline retention claim needs an estimator, uncertainty, and a jitter null model, and the Oracle and judge audits need actual reporting, before the paper's central quantitative case is established.

## Reviewer 2

- **Overall assessment**
This is an unusually candid workshop paper that instruments the question-by-question search of LLM agents on a verified 22-puzzle Turtle Soup set and reports its own null results (H2 rejected, H3 unsupported) without burying them. The engineering discipline (provenance-enforced puzzle set, cross-family Oracle, dual anchors, forced per-round checkpoints) is well above workshop norms. However, the central interpretive claim, that failure is ineffective exploration rather than budget or capability, is not the claim the evidence establishes; the paper's own results show capability moving sustained peaks 3.5-fold and the smallest model failing substantially by token exhaustion. The flagship "finding and losing" retention number (0.69 to 0.76) is a max-versus-endpoint statistic with no null model, so it may partly be a selection artifact of taking the maximum of a noisy series the paper itself documents as noisy. Of the two named patterns, only circling is actually read from geometry; abandoning is read from score dynamics and commitment counts, contradicting the contribution statement that both are identified jointly in geometry and language space. The paper is close to a solid descriptive contribution if the claims are re-scoped to what the instruments actually showed.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why**
Researchers in agent evaluation and interpretability who want process-level rather than endpoint-level diagnostics, since the checkpoint-forcing design and the worked single-game table are directly reusable ideas. Creativity-psychometrics researchers would also take interest, because the paper is a rare transfer of forward-flow-style semantic-travel measures to agents, including an informative failure of their predictive use.

- **Major strengths**
- Honest reporting of null and negative results (H2's absent drift signature, H3's within-puzzle null, the anchor sign-flips on 38/198 traces), with the anchor's circularity stated plainly rather than hidden.
- The per-round forced-checkpoint design is genuinely informative; the appendix worked game (round-10 committed story scoring 1.00, later abandoned) is the single most convincing piece of evidence in the paper.
- Provenance discipline on puzzles (human-solved only, quarantined generated family, code-enforced separation) and the Oracle audit prerequisite, including the cross-family requirement, address real validity threats other benchmark papers ignore.
- The clue-recall versus causal-logic dissociation (12.6/30 against 7.2/70 for 397B) is a clean, well-supported finding.

- **Major Concerns**

- **R2-M1.** Severity Major. Blocking Yes. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract finding (1) and Section 5.1 ("The plateau is an equilibrium of finding and losing"). Evidence pointer, Figure 1b; the 0.69 to 0.76 retention figure; Appendix B's own statement that rewording swings the clue half 70 to 0 between adjacent rounds. Concern, the retention statistic compares the maximum of roughly 29 sustained-checkpoint values to the endpoint value; the maximum of any noisy stationary series exceeds its endpoint in expectation, so some unquantified fraction of the "losing" is regression to the mean rather than abandonment. The two-round sustaining requirement mitigates but does not bound this. Why it matters, the "equilibrium of finding and losing" is the paper's explanation of the flat curve and the basis of the abandoning construct; if a stationary-noise null reproduces much of the 0.7 ratio, the central explanatory story weakens badly. Resolution test, report the same retention statistic under a null (for example, checkpoint sequences with rounds permuted within game, or a simulated stationary series matched to per-model checkpoint variance) and show observed retention is significantly below the null's.

- **R2-M2.** Severity Major. Blocking Yes. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract ("the flat accuracy curve is not a resource limit") and Contribution (1) ("driven neither by round budget nor by model capability alone, but by ineffective exploration"). Evidence pointer, Section 5.1 (sustained peak 0.084 versus 0.296 across scale), Section 5.2 (175 of 396 4B games end by token-budget exhaustion), Section 5.3 (within-model geometry does not predict outcome, LRT p >= 0.20). Concern, the evidence shows capability strongly determines what is found, and shows the smallest model's failures are substantially literal resource failures (token exhaustion), while the exploration measures themselves carry no within-puzzle predictive power. "Ineffective exploration" is asserted as the driver without any intervention (for example, forcing commitment at the sustained peak, or injecting question diversity) that would demonstrate exploration is causally load-bearing. The H3 null actively undercuts the causal reading the abstract sells. Why it matters, this is the headline claim; as written the abstract oversells an interpretation as a finding. Resolution test, either run one intervention that manipulates exploration or commitment and moves outcome, or rewrite the abstract and Contribution (1) to the defensible descriptive form (capability raises peaks, retention is scale-invariant, and the geometry describes but does not predict failure).

- **R2-M3.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis originality. Claim pointer, Contribution (2), "two behavioral patterns... identified jointly in geometry space and in language space," and abstract finding (2) "read jointly from geometry and language." Evidence pointer, Section 5.3, which states "Abandoning is invisible to lexical measures altogether; it shows in commitment behavior and checkpoint dynamics," and grounds it in 4 to 6 of 66 games per larger model. Concern, abandoning is identified in neither geometry nor language space; it is a score-trajectory and commitment phenomenon, and it rests on a handful of games per model. The contribution statement and abstract therefore mischaracterize the paper's own evidence, and the symmetric two-pattern taxonomy is presented as more instrument-derived than it is. Why it matters, the paper's claimed novelty is the trajectory instrument; if one of the two patterns is found by ordinary score inspection, the instrument's contribution shrinks to one pattern. Resolution test, restate where each pattern is actually detected, report how many games per model exhibit each pattern under a stated criterion, and either show a geometric or lexical signature of abandoning or stop claiming one.

- **R2-M4.** Severity Major. Blocking Yes. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, Section 1 ("The results confirm H1 (as circling)") and Section 5.3's circling attribution to 4B. Evidence pointer, Section 5.2 (175/396 4B games end by token-budget exhaustion) together with Section 6's harness trap ("truncated chain-of-thought fragments to which every Oracle reply is 'irrelevant'... indistinguishable from an agent that cannot form a question"). Concern, the paper names exactly the artifact that could manufacture 4B's low stride and non-commitment (degenerate or truncated questions under decoding pressure) but never states whether the reported 4B grid was checked against it. If a material fraction of 4B questions are malformed, "circling" is a decoding artifact, not a search behavior, and H1's confirmation is void for the one model said to exhibit it. Separately, the qualitative fresh-words re-asking evidence in the appendix comes from a 397B game, so language-space evidence of 4B circling is never shown. Why it matters, circling is one of the two central constructs and the basis of the "opposite interventions" conclusion. Resolution test, report the fraction of 4B questions that are well-formed yes/no questions in the analyzed grid, recompute stride excluding malformed rounds, and show at least one 4B transcript excerpt exhibiting same-hypothesis re-asks in fresh words.

- **R2-M5.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, all "at every scale" and "scale raises... but not..." statements (abstract, Sections 1, 5.1, 5.3). Evidence pointer, Section 5's model roster, Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.6-27B, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B. Concern, the ladder mixes model generations (3.5 and 3.6) and architectures (the 397B is an A17B mixture), with n = 3 models and 3 seeds, yet conclusions are phrased as scale laws. The 27B, the off-generation model, is also the outlier on stride (0.114, above the 397B's 0.088) and on commitment, which is at least as consistent with a generation or training-recipe effect as with scale. Why it matters, "scaling does not remove it" is a strong claim on three confounded points. Resolution test, soften scale language to per-model language throughout, or add at least one same-generation size pair and show the pattern holds.

- **R2-M6.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis originality. Claim pointer, Section 2's delta statements, chiefly "They score the destination; we score the trajectory," and the naming of circling and abandoning as new patterns. Evidence pointer, Section 2 as written; the cited TurtleSoup-Bench title itself ("What to Ask Next? Probing the Imaginative Reasoning of LLMs"). Concern, the positioning against the closest prior work is one sentence per system, and at least one cited benchmark by its own framing probes the question-asking process, so the destination-versus-trajectory contrast needs substantiation rather than assertion. Likewise, circling and abandoning are presented as discovered constructs without discussion of whether they relabel familiar phenomena (repetitive-question degeneration and failure to commit or retain a correct answer are both folk-known failure modes in multi-turn agents); the paper cites no agent-behavior literature outside turtle-soup benchmarks and creativity psychometrics against which to establish the delta. Whether specific prior work anticipates these constructs is not assessable from the packet, but the burden of differentiation sits with the paper and is currently unmet. Why it matters, at a venue about interpreting agent behavior, the named constructs are the product; if they are relabels, the contribution reduces to the harness and dataset. Resolution test, add a positioning paragraph that states precisely what TurtleSoup-Bench measures about the question process and what the trajectory geometry adds, and situates circling and abandoning against existing failure-mode vocabulary.

- **R2-M7.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, abstract finding (4), "an unaudited Oracle collapses questions to 'irrelevant' and makes every game unwinnable." Evidence pointer, Section 4's prerequisite paragraph; Section 5 reports results only for the audited DeepSeek-V3.1 Oracle. Concern, the abstract states an empirical finding (every game unwinnable under an unaudited Oracle) for which the Results section contains no experiment, no candidate model named, no game counts, and no scores. As written it is a design motivation promoted to a finding. Why it matters, an abstract's numbered findings must each be backed by reported results; this one is currently unfalsifiable from the paper. Resolution test, report the audit experiment (candidates, held-out pair accuracy, and at least one played grid or pilot under a failing Oracle), or remove it from the abstract's findings list and keep it as methodology.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
R2-M1 (null model for the retention statistic), R2-M2 (either an exploration intervention or a re-scoped central claim), and R2-M4 (verification that 4B's circling is not a decoding artifact, plus 4B language-space evidence). Until these three are resolved, the paper's explanatory story (flatness explained by ineffective exploration, expressed as circling and abandoning) is not established; R2-M5 and R2-M7 must also be fixed but are wording and reporting repairs rather than new evidence.

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
Originality is moderate; the transfer of forward-flow-style semantic-travel measures to interactive agents is a genuinely fresh instrument, but its predictive use fails by the paper's own tests, and the two named behavioral constructs are not yet differentiated from familiar failure modes, so the surviving novelty is the harness, the provenance-verified puzzle set, and the checkpoint-forcing design. Importance is real for the agent-evaluation community, since process-level diagnostics are scarce, but is capped while the central claim outruns the evidence. Soundness is mixed; the grid is clean and the nulls honestly reported, yet the retention statistic lacks a null model, the circling attribution is exposed to a decoding artifact the paper itself describes, and the scale framing rests on three confounded models. Readability is good in structure and often vivid in prose, but untranslated Chinese in the key table, an interpretive figure title, and residual draft scaffolding need repair.

- **Recommendation posture**
Major revision needed. The descriptive core (capability raises peaks while retention stays flat, the score-half dissociation, the checkpoint-forcing methodology, and the worked-game evidence) is worth presenting at this workshop, but the abstract and contributions currently claim an explanatory result the experiments do not establish, and two of the load-bearing analyses (retention, circling) need artifact controls before the two-pattern story can stand.

## Reviewer 3

- **Overall assessment** This is an unusually candid and well-motivated workshop submission. It reframes an interactive benchmark from endpoint scoring to process instrumentation, reports its own negative result (H3) plainly, and treats environment validity (the Oracle audit, provenance-enforced puzzles, a non-circular difficulty covariate) as a first-class methodological concern, which fits the IAB brief well. However, from a methodology and reproducibility standpoint the paper is not yet self-supporting. The core association-trajectory pipeline is unspecified at the level a reader would need to re-run or even sanity-check it, the headline claim that failure "is not a resource limit" sits in unresolved tension with the paper's own report that 175 of 396 small-model games ended by token exhaustion and with its own named harness trap, and there is an unexplained internal discrepancy between E1 and E2 at matched round 30 for the 4B model. The Chinese-language material, including the load-bearing worked example, is not accessible to most of the NeurIPS audience. All of these look fixable within a revision cycle.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers building agent benchmarks and evaluation harnesses, because the Oracle-audit and cross-family requirements and the provenance-enforced puzzle set are directly reusable design lessons. Interpretability-of-behavior researchers, because the paper offers a text-only, activation-free instrument for search dynamics and an honest account of where it does and does not predict. There is also a smaller crossover audience in computational creativity and psychometrics, given the explicit adaptation of forward flow and the DAT.

- **Major strengths**
  - The finding-versus-keeping decomposition (sustained peak, retention of 0.69 to 0.76 unchanged by scale) is a genuinely process-level observation that endpoint metrics cannot produce, and Figure 1b communicates it clearly.
  - Honest negative-result reporting; H3's failure is stated in the abstract, the between-model versus within-model correlation distinction (pooled rho 0.23 vanishing within model) is exactly the right analysis, and both anchors are reported as promised.
  - The environment-validity stance is exemplary in intent; the Oracle audit as a prerequisite, the cross-family requirement, the quarantined generated puzzle family with tests, and the memorization caveat are all stated rather than hidden.
  - The under-determination measure is designed to avoid circularity with the accuracy-versus-rounds analysis, and the paper checks it against the obvious surrogate (surface length).
  - The two-subscore dissociation (logic earned disproportionately to clue recall for every model) is a concrete validation that the judge half is not redundant.
  - The worked game in Appendix B, with verbatim log rows and a round-10 committed story that is the solution, is the right kind of evidence for the "abandoning" pattern, subject to the accessibility concern below.

- **Major Concerns**

  - **R3-M1.** Severity Major. Blocking Yes. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, Abstract finding (1) and Contribution (1) ("the flat accuracy curve is not a resource limit"; "task failure is driven neither by round budget nor by model capability alone"); Section 5.2; Section 6 harness-trap paragraph. Evidence pointer, Section 5.2 reports 175 of 396 4B games ending "by token-budget exhaustion rather than by answer"; Section 6 states that under too small a token budget a model's logged questions are truncated chain-of-thought fragments, "indistinguishable from an agent that cannot form a question"; no token budget, decoding configuration, or truncation audit is reported anywhere. Concern, the paper's own numbers show the smallest model hitting a literal resource limit in 44 percent of E2 games, and the paper's own limitations section names a trap under which such games are uninterpretable from transcripts. Yet the 4B results carry half of the behavioral story (circling, stride 0.030, 2 of 66 commits), and no check is reported that the E1 games feeding the geometry analysis were free of truncated-question artifacts, nor is the token budget itself stated. Why it matters, if a material fraction of 4B questions are truncated fragments answered "irrelevant", then low stride, non-commitment, and the monotonic harm of longer caps could all be decoding artifacts of the harness rather than a behavioral pattern of the model, which would undercut the circling half of Contribution (2) and the "not a resource limit" framing of Contribution (1). Resolution test, state the per-turn and per-game token budgets and decoding configuration; report, for each model in E1, the fraction of rounds whose logged question was a well-formed yes/no question versus a truncated fragment (a rubric plus sampling suffices); re-report the 4B stride and commitment statistics excluding affected games, or show they are unaffected.

  - **R3-M2.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, Section 3.4 (association-trajectory instrumentation); Section 3.2 and Appendix A (under-determination); all stride and drift numbers in Section 5.3 and Figure 2. Evidence pointer, Section 3.4 says only "we extract the question's keywords, embed them, and aggregate them to a unit vector"; no keyword-extraction method, no embedding model, no definition of the distance d, no aggregation rule, and no statement of how the anchor set A is turned into a point or set for computing h_t. Appendix A does not name the model that writes the twelve cold guesses, its sampling settings, or the metric by which "closest" is judged. Concern, every quantitative geometric claim in the paper depends on a pipeline whose components are unspecified. Why it matters, the instrument is the paper's central contribution; as written it is not reproducible from the text, and the promised harness release is asserted without an availability statement. Resolution test, specify extractor, embedding model and version, distance function, keyword-to-vector aggregation, anchor construction for both anchor variants, and the cold-guess generator, settings, and closeness metric for under-determination; add an artifact availability statement.

  - **R3-M3.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, Abstract ("three Qwen models (4B, 27B, 397B)"; "a retention scale leaves unchanged even as it raises the peaks 3.5-fold"); Section 5 preamble; all "scale" language in Sections 5.1 to 5.3. Evidence pointer, Section 5 names the grid as Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.6-27B, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B. Concern, the three models are not a clean scale sweep. The 27B model is from a different generation (3.6 versus 3.5), and the 397B model is a mixture-of-experts with 17B active parameters, which is not straightforwardly "larger" than a 27B dense model along the axis the prose implies. The 27B model in fact has the largest mean stride (0.114) and the most volunteered commitments, which the tier framing quietly absorbs. Why it matters, claims of the form "scale raises the peaks but not retention" attribute differences to parameter count when generation, architecture, and post-training may differ. Evidence pointer, no section reports the audit protocol or any data from a failed Oracle candidate; the same Section 5 sentence assigns DeepSeek-V3.1 both the Oracle and the logic-judge roles. Concern, a headline abstract finding is currently supported by no reported experiment, and the one number attached to the audit ("95%") is undefined. Separately, the Oracle and the judge share a model, so any DeepSeek-specific reading of the hidden story shapes both the evidence stream and the logic scoring; the cross-family rule is enforced against the Questioner but not between the two environment roles. Why it matters, the paper's own thesis is that agent claims must begin with an information audit of the environment; the environment described here must meet the standard the paper sets. Resolution test, report the audit protocol and the rejected-Oracle data behind finding (4), define the 95% figure, and either justify or diversify the shared Oracle-judge model (a judge-agreement check against a second family would suffice).

  - **R3-M5.** Severity Major. Blocking No. Axis technical soundness. Claim pointer, Abstract finding (3) ("no predictive power... p >= 0.20"); Section 5.3 mixed-effects paragraphs and both-anchors paragraph. Evidence pointer, Section 5.3 gives LRT p-values (0.39, 0.20, 0.22) and the surface-only anchor LRT p = 0.10; the response variable, model family, and random-effects structure beyond a puzzle intercept are unstated; Figure 1a's caption computes 95% CI bands over 66 games. ## Cross-review synthesis (post-review; not shown to reviewers)

- **Consensus strengths** All three reviewers independently praise the honest null-result reporting (H2, H3, anchor sign-flips), the per-round forced-checkpoint instrument with the Appendix B worked game as the paper's most convincing evidence, the provenance-enforced puzzle set, the cross-family Oracle-audit stance, and the finding-versus-keeping decomposition as a genuinely process-level observation. All three recommend major revision while judging the work worth publishing at this workshop.

- **Consensus blocking concerns**
  1. **S1. The retention statistic needs an estimator, uncertainty, and a jitter null model** (R1-M1 + R2-M1 + R3-m2, blocking for R1 and R2). A max-versus-endpoint statistic on a series the paper itself documents as noisy is upward-biased; without a within-game permutation or stationary-noise null, "finding and losing" is not distinguishable from measurement noise around a stable plateau.
  2. **S2. The 4B behavioral story must be cleared of the token-truncation artifact the paper itself names** (R2-M4 + R3-M1 + R1-M4, blocking for R2 and R3). State token budgets and decoding configuration; report question well-formedness by model; recompute 4B stride/commitment excluding affected games; condition the E2 decline on non-exhausted games.
  3. **S3. The causal headline ("failure is ineffective exploration, not budget or capability") outruns the evidence** (R2-M2 + R3-M1 + R1's overall assessment, blocking for R2). Either add an intervention or re-scope the abstract and Contribution (1) to the defensible descriptive form.
  4. **S4. The Oracle/judge audit must actually be reported, and abstract finding (4) demoted or evidenced** (R1-M2 + R2-M7 + R3-M4, blocking for R1). Protocol, denominator, per-class accuracy, a rejected candidate's numbers; note the shared Oracle-judge model.

- **Other consensus major concerns**
  - **S5. Scale language on three confounded models** (R1-m5 + R2-M5 + R3-M3): soften to model/tier language, note the generation and MoE confounds.
  - **S6. The two-pattern taxonomy is not operationalized per game, and "identified jointly in geometry and language space" mischaracterizes abandoning** (R1-M5 + R2-M3): define per-game criteria, report counts per model, restate where each pattern is detected, reconcile the 4B below-half-peak count.
  - **S7. The mixed model is underspecified and its null overstated; the abstract's "p >= 0.20" is contradicted by the surface-anchor p = 0.10** (R1-M3 + R3-M5): specify response/estimation/random effects, add the stride main-effect test and coefficient CIs, correct the abstract.
  - **S8. Chinese-language material blocks the key evidence for most readers** (R2-m2 + R3-M7, major for R3): gloss terms, translate Table 1, add the rows the text analyzes.
  - **S9. Pipeline and audit reproducibility** (R3-M2 + R1-m3 + R2-m4): specify extractor, encoder, distance, aggregation, anchor construction, cold-guess generator; add an artifact availability statement.
  - **S10. The subscore-dissociation claim needs the correlation it implies** (R1-M6 + R2-m5): report per-game correlation between halves and judge inter-sample agreement; temper "demonstrably different competences" if needed.

- **Where emphasis differs across reviewers** R3 alone surfaces the E1-versus-E2 matched-round-30 discrepancy for 4B (0.05 vs 0.004) and elevates checkpoint-protocol reactivity to a major concern; this is a genuine single-reviewer finding worth treating as major. R2 alone presses the originality axis (positioning against TurtleSoup-Bench, whether circling/abandoning relabel known failure modes). R1 alone presses estimator-level statistics (per-game ratio instability, early-commit handling, score quantization).

- **Minor revision checklist** Strip draft scaffolding and the TBD author note (3×); translate/gloss Chinese (2×); retitle Figure 2a descriptively (R2-m6); symmetric axis and anchor label for Figure 2b (R3-m3); define "failed game" vs exhaustion (R1-m6); state early-commit retention rule (R1-m7); score-granularity histogram (R1-m4); memorization probe or caveat (R3-m4); artifact statement (R3-m5); judge settings and agreement (R3-m6); justify or relativize the 70/30 weights (R2-m5); verify 4-page fit (R2-m7).

- **Broad-interest / significance readout** The paper's durable contributions, per all three reviewers, are the checkpoint-forcing instrument, the provenance-verified puzzle set with a non-circular difficulty measure, the environment-audit doctrine, and the honest negative results; the two-pattern vocabulary is promising but must be operationalized and artifact-controlled before it can carry the concluding claim.

- **Most important issues to resolve before a strong case is established** S1 (retention null model), S2 (truncation artifact audit), S3 (re-scoped central claim), S4 (audit reporting), then S6/S7 (taxonomy operationalization and statistical specification), then S8 (translation).
